A tidal sea cave on the southern coast, mouth opening just beside where the great sea-beast washed ashore and rotted into bone. The cave only opens at low tide; at high water its entrance is swallowed by the surf.

The mouth was strung with a tripwire alarm — a fishing net staked taut across the entrance, lashed with cookware, bone, and the broken haft of a spear. The Tidefang's crew had rigged it to warn them of anything coming in from outside. It never sounded. Whatever came for them had not come from the sea.

**Entrance pool.** Past the mouth the floor slopes down to a shallow pool of trapped seawater. Giant crabs nest here, and at least one giant spider drops from threads on the ceiling. Past the pool, the Tidefang itself sits half-afloat in the shallows — a humble fishing vessel, sails frayed but mast standing — alongside the cold ashes of a long-abandoned camp.

**The ship.** Five neatly stacked bedrolls on the deck. A locked captain's quarters at the rear, holding a desk, an unlit lantern, and a logbook in Draconic. A door in the floor. Bookshelves with a waterproof scroll case and a charm necklace. Behind the boat, two cairns built against the cave wall — bedrolls smoothed flat under stacked stones, names scratched in charred Draconic on the rock above. No bodies; only names.

**The deep chamber.** Past the camp the cave narrows and opens into a chamber where all sound falls away. Bioluminescent moss casts the walls in shifting blues and greens; a wide pool of still black water reflects the glow like a dark mirror. Five lizardfolk lay dead here — four on the shore, one face-down in the water — bearing massive crushing injuries and pus-filled nodules. A chuul guarded the pool, a thrall of the aboleth.

The strange quiet in the chamber was not magical. Ribbons of a soft, oily, extraordinarily dense mineral threaded through the floor and walls — galena, rich in lead. The same shielding kept the aboleth's psychic call dulled here. A refuge that became a tomb.

The party recovered the Tidefang and its dead and sailed the old fishing vessel back along the coast to N.O.D.E. camp.
