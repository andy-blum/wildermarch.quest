A vast, ancient pyramid buried beneath the desert, descending into the earth from beneath Auntie Dirtha Bonebender's shack. Its apex sits in the sand of an enormous underground cavern; the structure itself plunges far deeper.

**The Cavern:**
The oasis pool is no pool at all — it drops away into a deep vertical shaft, a well plunging into blackness beneath the desert. The shaft is patrolled by Dirtha's eels and emerges into open air through a thundering waterfall that feeds a vast underground lake. Stalagmites and stalactites of brown desert stone frame the chamber, and an ambient glow softens the darkness enough to see without other light.

**Dirtha's Shack:**
On the far bank past a dune of sand sits a small shack adorned with animal bones, with a bubbling cauldron out front producing a smell like durian, dirty socks, and burning refuse. Dirtha can stop the waterfall with a snap of her fingers. The shack does not sit on stone — it sits on the apex of the pyramid, with the entrance leading down into the dark below.

**The Chambers:**
The pyramid's interior is a warren of chambers, each sealed and tested:

- **The Scarab Corridor:** A narrow sandstone passage lined with torches that ignite as the party passes. Black scarabs cling to the ceiling and walls, their shadows moving against the flame. The air smells sweet, like an old auntie's house.

- **The Circle of Weights:** A chamber dominated by a great stone disc inscribed with the words *"Weigh what was given. Weigh what was taken. Weigh what was promised. Weigh what was broken."* Six alcoves hold offerings: a golden feather, a clay heart, a vial of dark liquid, a bronze coin, a linen scroll, and a broken obsidian mirror. The correct arrangement unlocks the descent to deeper levels.

- **The Hourglass Room:** A chamber containing a plinth with three relics: a feather quill with its tip snapped intentionally, a bowl of obsidian glass filled with impossibly smooth white sand that drains when a mirror-polished sword blade is fitted to a groove on the plinth, and runes that name the words for things. The sand drains into channels beneath the floor, and the chamber's breeze carries the voices of the desert—old voices, remembering.

- **The Tomb of Records:** A resting place for two undead guardians — a priest holding the marriage contract of Ramkheret and She Who Speaks to Empty Places, and a soldier with an empty gift box bearing a riddle.

- **The Hall of Sphinxes:** Twin golden sphinxes named Sekahnu (amber-eyed) and Vetherek (blue-eyed) guard the descent. One always lies; one always tells the truth. The liar's puzzle must be solved to proceed.

- **The Sarcophagus:** An open tomb containing the journal of King Ramkheret, inscribed in Fey script and written partly in his own hand and partly in the hand of his immortal consort. The tomb itself is stripped of any mortal remains.

- **The Cat's Chamber:** At the deepest known level, six clay cat statues sit in silent poses, and a long black cat with amber eyes occupies the center of the room. It speaks in all languages at once, and its presence is old — older than the pyramid itself.

**The Empty Throne:**
When Dirtha drew the Judgment card during her bone-card reading, her voice shifted — lower, older — and she spoke of "the Empty Throne. I was waiting. I was always waiting. Lucky for you, they sent a herald." Whatever waits in the depths beyond the cat's chamber is something she serves.

When Dirtha vanished into the wind, the sand began to strip away from the cavern floor grain by grain, revealing the pyramid that had been buried beneath it.
